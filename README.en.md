## International Organizations and Regional Systems

### International Organizations

- WIPO PATENTSCOPE: <https://patentscope.wipo.int/search/en/search.jsf> — the official search system of the World Intellectual Property Organization. The best place to search PCT international applications, and it also covers patent data from many countries and regions. Its functions are fairly complete and browsing documents is convenient. Highly recommended.
- WIPO Global Design Database: <https://www.wipo.int/designdb/en/> — WIPO's design search database, bringing together Hague System registrations and design data from member states. You can search by product name, Locarno classification, holder and other fields.

### Regional Organizations

- European Patent Office (EPO) Espacenet: <https://worldwide.espacenet.com/> — the free database with the widest coverage of countries and regions, covering patent documents from more than 100 countries and regions with dozens of language interfaces. It shows families, citations and legal status and supports IPC and CPC classification search. The only field in which Europe rules the world. Most recommended.
- European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO): <https://euipo.europa.eu> — Europe and the EU, easy to confuse. It handles EU trade marks and registered Community designs, and its eSearch plus (trade marks, designs, owners, bulletins) and eSearch Case Law tools are free. Designs only for now, so of limited value.
- Eurasian Patent Organization (EAPO): <https://www.eapo.org> — a grand-sounding name; is it going to unify the European and Asian IP offices? It is a regional organization of Russia and other Eurasian countries that receives and grants Eurasian patents, and its website offers the ЕАПАТИС (EAPATIS) search system and bulletins.
- African Regional Intellectual Property Organization (ARIPO): <http://www.aripo.org> — the regional IP organization of English-speaking Africa, receiving patent, utility model, trade mark and industrial design applications on behalf of its member states. Its website offers the Regional IP Database and official journals.
- African Intellectual Property Organization (OAPI): <https://oapi.int/> — the regional IP organization of French-speaking Africa, receiving patent, trade mark and industrial design applications from its member states under the Bangui Agreement. Its website provides member information and official bulletins.
- Gulf Cooperation Council Patent Office (GCCPO): <http://www.gccpo.org> — handles patent grants for the GCC member states (Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman). You can search granted patents from 1998 onwards; quick search, advanced search and the patent gazette are available, mostly in Arabic with an English interface as well.
- Benelux Office for Intellectual Property (BOIP): <https://www.boip.int> — who would have thought Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg would set up an intellectual property organization of their own. It registers Benelux trade marks and designs, and its registers can be searched free of charge.
- European Publication Server (EPO): <https://data.epo.org/publication-server/> — the EPO's official platform for publishing and downloading European patent documents, with monthly full-text downloads.

## Asia

### Armenia
- Intellectual Property Office of Armenia: <https://aipo.am> — an agency under the Ministry of Economy that grants invention and industrial design patents and registers trade marks and geographical indications. The website is available in Armenian, Russian and English.
- Inventions and utility models: <https://aipo.am/hy/registered-by-national-procedure> — covers nationally registered inventions and utility models from registration No. 1 onwards, plus inventions registered with EAPO and valid in Armenia, with bibliographic data and full text.
- Industrial designs: <https://aipo.am/hy/search-int-classification> — designs from registration No. 1 onwards, with bibliographic data and drawings; the interface supports Armenian, Russian and English.
- Official bulletin: <https://aipo.am/hy/pages/show/official-bulletin> — bulletins from 1995 onwards covering the grant of patents and industrial designs and design renewals, in Armenian, Russian and English.

### Azerbaijan
- AzSTAND (standardization and patents): <http://www.azstand.gov.az> — the website now focuses on standardization and metrology; the patent search entry only supports look-up by registration number, not keyword or classification search across the whole collection.

### Bangladesh
- Department of Patents, Designs and Trademarks (DPDT): <https://www.dpdt.gov.bd> — an agency under the Ministry of Industries responsible for patents, industrial designs, trade marks and geographical indications; the website is in Bengali and English.
- Published applications: <https://www.dpdt.gov.bd/site/page/50e5ef29-4df7-4278-b6c8-0547d8c2c31c/-#> — applications filed since December 2022, with bibliographic data, abstracts and drawings.
- Granted patents: <https://www.dpdt.gov.bd/site/page/72eeeb40-236f-49b3-a753-c6598effe3a3/-> — granted patent documents since November 2022, with bibliographic data, abstracts and drawings.
- Industrial designs: <https://www.dpdt.gov.bd/site/page/66fe413f-d5fd-41dc-9f65-2a9a3c9270dd/-> — design documents since April 2024, with bibliographic data and images or photographs.
- Patent gazette: <https://www.dpdt.gov.bd/site/page/ddda49a0-dcb2-497a-bc66-b373ebea2e76/-> — application gazettes from May 2014 to November 2022 and granted patent gazettes from July 2014 to November 2022.

### Brunei
- Brunei Intellectual Property Office (BruIPO): <http://www.bruipo.gov.bn> — the government department responsible for registering patents, trade marks, industrial designs and plant varieties in Brunei; the website is in English.
- IP search system: <http://ipsearch.bruipo.gov.bn> — searches Brunei patents, trade marks and industrial designs (excluding designs registered through the Hague System). Results include bibliographic data, legal status and drawings; the platform supports Malay, English, French and Japanese.

### China
- China National Intellectual Property Administration (CNIPA): <https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/> — the official website, publishing policies and regulations, statistics, business services and patent information, in Chinese and English.
- China Patent Search and Analysis System: <https://pss-system.cponline.cnipa.gov.cn/conventionalSearch> — CNIPA's free public search and analysis system, covering patent data from more than 100 countries, regions and organizations, with family, citation and legal status information in nine languages including Chinese, English and German. A rare free official search and analysis tool in China.
- Design Patent Search Public Service System: <https://d.cnipa.gov.cn/micro-portal/#/login> — brings together design data from China, the United States, the European Union, Japan and the Republic of Korea, searchable by application number, publication number, applicant, product name, main classification and more. Very useful for design searches.
- China Patent Publication and Announcement System: <http://epub.cnipa.gov.cn/> — the official channel for Chinese patent publications, covering all Chinese patent publications since September 1985, with gazettes and transaction data. For Chinese patents no other database is more up to date; no ads and no registration required, but the user experience is poor. Useful for downloading the occasional newly published Chinese patent that other databases have not yet indexed.
- China and Multi-Country Patent Examination Information Query System: <https://cpquery.cponline.cnipa.gov.cn> — the official CNIPA website for the application, examination, fee, correspondence, announcement and family information of Chinese patents. It also covers examination information from the EPO, the USPTO, the JPO and KIPO, in eight languages. The user experience is dreadful, but there is no way around it.
- CNIPR (China Intellectual Property Network): <http://search.cnipr.com/pages!advSearch.action> — a patent information service platform run by the Intellectual Property Publishing House (under CNIPA) since 1999. It offers simple, expression, lapsed-patent and legal-status search, family and citation information, Chinese full-text downloads and number-list batch search (up to 2,000 numbers per query). Accounts range from free registration to paid standard and premium tiers; without logging in you can barely use it, and the user experience is poor.
- Beijing IP Public Information Platform: <http://www.beijingip.cn/jopm_ww/website/index.do> — a public-interest platform run by the Beijing Intellectual Property Office, covering more than 140 million patent records (50.1 million Chinese patents as of December 2024) with citations, families and legal status. It offers patent search, IPC search, legal status search, patent analysis and online translation, most of which can be used without logging in, though the interface still bears the marks of a government-built product.
- National Key Industry Patent Information Service Platform: <http://chinaip.cnipa.gov.cn> — run by CNIPA, covering more than 150 million patents from 105 countries, regions and organizations plus Chinese legal status data. It is organized into more than 30 industry topics such as brain science, network communication, hydrogen and energy storage, new energy vehicles, chips, artificial intelligence, traditional Chinese medicine and the seed industry, and offers basic and advanced search, classification navigation, legal status search, statistical analysis and machine translation. Free and no registration required.
- China Patent Technology Development Co., Ltd.: <https://www.cnpatent.com> — the website of a company under CNIPA. It mainly provides IPC and CPC classification, deep-processed patent data and search consultancy; it is no longer a free public search platform.
- PatentStar: <https://www.patentstar.com.cn> — run by the China Patent Information Center under CNIPA and formerly known as the CPRS system. It covers more than 100 million patents from 105 countries, regions and organizations, with smart search, table search, expert search, number-list search, legal status, statistical analysis and batch download. It asks you to register the moment you arrive; never tried it, so no comment.
- CNKI (China National Knowledge Infrastructure): <https://kns.cnki.net/kns8s/?classid=VUDIXAIY> — CNKI's patent database, licensed by the Intellectual Property Publishing House and covering Chinese patent full texts since 1985. It sits on the same platform as journals and dissertations — patent data is everywhere. An institutional account or login is required to download full texts.
- Wanfang Data: <https://c.wanfangdata.com.cn/patent> — Wanfang's Chinese and foreign patent database with about 156 million patent records, including more than 40.6 million Chinese patent full texts since 1985 (synced with CNIPA, about three million new records a year) and more than 110 million foreign patent records covering eleven countries (China, the US, Japan, the UK, Germany, France, Switzerland, Russia, Korea, Canada and Australia) and two organizations (WIPO and the EPO), with links to EPO full texts. Login or an institutional account is required.
- Duxiu (Chaoxing): <http://www.duxiu.com/?channel=searchPatent> — the patent channel of Chaoxing's Duxiu academic search. Chinese patents only; it requires institutional IP authorization or a Duxiu account, and IPs outside the service range are asked to log in.
- DrugFuture: <http://www.drugfuture.com> — free downloads of full-text patent specifications for China (since 1985), the United States (since 1790) and Europe. Search by application or publication number and download PDF or packaged TIF files without registration. A common stop for pharmacy-related patent searching.
- National Science and Technology Library (NSTL): <https://www.nstl.gov.cn/search.html> — searches journals, papers, books and other literature as well as patents, and supports searching global patents in Chinese. Searching and exporting bibliographic records are free; full texts are obtained through a paid document delivery service.

### China, Hong Kong SAR
- Hong Kong Intellectual Property Department (IPD) search system: <https://esearch.ipd.gov.hk/nis-pos-view/pt> — the online search system of the IPD of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Government, covering Hong Kong trade marks, patents and designs, with an additional trade mark image search tool. The interface is in Traditional Chinese and English.

### China, Macao SAR
- Macao Economic and Technological Development Bureau (DSEDT): <https://www.dsedt.gov.mo> — industrial property registration in Macao is handled by the DSEDT. The website offers searches of trade mark, invention patent, utility patent, design and utility model registrations, as well as annuity, certificate and search report status enquiries, in Traditional Chinese, Simplified Chinese, Portuguese and English.

### Taiwan, China
- Taiwan patent search system: <https://tiponet.tipo.gov.tw/gpss/> — the Global Patent Search System (GPSS) of the Intellectual Property Office of the Taiwan region (the old gpss.tipo.gov.tw was closed in December 2024). It covers patents of the Taiwan region and data from the five offices of the US, Japan, Europe, Korea and mainland China since 2006; free registration gives access to statistical chart analysis. The interface is in Traditional Chinese. I will not call it the "Republic of China" patent search system.

### Georgia
- National Intellectual Property Center of Georgia (Sakpatenti): <http://www.sakpatenti.org.ge> — the authority for patents, trade marks and industrial designs in Georgia. Its website provides access to the state register with patents from 2008 onwards, in Georgian and English.

### Indonesia
- Directorate General of Intellectual Property (DGIP): <https://www.dgip.go.id> — under the Ministry of Law, responsible for the examination of patents, industrial designs and trade marks; the website is in Indonesian and English.
- Patent and industrial design search: <https://pdki-indonesia.dgip.go.id/> — an online database in Indonesian covering patents and industrial designs, with bibliographic data and full texts.
- Official gazette (patents): <https://www.dgip.go.id/berita-resmi/berita-resmi-paten> — patent gazettes published since 2020, updated weekly, with published applications and granted patents.
- Official gazette (industrial designs): <https://www.dgip.go.id/berita-resmi/berita-resmi-desain-industri> — design gazettes published since 2020, updated weekly, with the relevant applications and grants.

### Iraq
- Central Organization for Standardization and Quality Control (COSQC): <https://www.cosqc.gov.iq/> — under the Ministry of Planning, with a patents and industrial designs department responsible for industrial property protection; the website is in Arabic.

### Japan
- Japan Patent Office database (J-PlatPat): <https://www.j-platpat.inpit.go.jp/web/all/top/BTmTopEnglishPage> — the JPO's official search platform for Japanese patents, utility models, designs and trade marks, and it also covers data from some other countries. English interface and machine translation are available.

### Kazakhstan
- National Institute of Intellectual Property (Qazpatent): <https://qazpatent.kz/kz> — an agency under the Ministry of Justice responsible for the examination and grant of inventions, utility models, industrial designs, trade marks, geographical indications and integrated circuit layouts; the website is in Kazakh, Russian and English.
- State Register of Industrial Property: <https://gosreestr.kazpatent.kz> — searches inventions, utility models, industrial designs and other registered industrial property, with Kazakh, Russian and English versions.
- Electronic bulletin: <https://qazpatent.kz/kz/electronic-bulletin> — electronic bulletins since 2018 with bibliographic data, abstracts and drawings.

### Kuwait
- Gulf Cooperation Council Patent Office (GCCPO): <http://www.gccpo.org> — patents in Kuwait are granted through the GCC Patent Office. You can view abstract-level records online and download PDF files.

### Malaysia
- Intellectual Property Corporation of Malaysia (MyIPO): <https://www.myipo.gov.my> — responsible for the search and examination of patents, trade marks, industrial designs and geographical indications; the website is in Malay and English.
- Online search and filing system: <https://iponlineext.myipo.gov.my/SPHI/Extra/Default.aspx> — searches inventions, utility models and industrial designs, with bibliographic data, prosecution history and design drawings; the interface is in English.
- IP online journal: <https://ipjournal.myipo.gov.my/ipjournal/index.cfm> — the official gazette for Malaysian patents, trade marks, industrial designs and geographical indications, in English.

### Mongolia
- Intellectual Property Office of Mongolia (IPOM): <http://www.ipom.gov.mn> — responsible for the examination and protection of invention patents, utility models, designs and trade marks; the website is in Mongolian and English.
- Inventions and utility models: <https://publish.ipom.mn/wopublish-search> — the IPOM Publish system searches invention patents and utility models in Mongolian and English, with bibliographic data, application type, legal status and drawings.
- Industrial designs: <https://publish.ipom.mn/wopublish-search/public/designs> — the IPOM Publish system also covers designs, with bibliographic data, application type, legal status, images or photographs.

### Nepal
- Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Supplies: <https://doind.gov.np> — carries out industrial development policy and regulation, industrial property protection and technology transfer; the website is in Nepali and English.
- Patents and industrial designs: <http://www.iponepal.gov.np/search.php> — the patent and industrial design search entry on the Department of Industry website, in English.
- Industrial property bulletin: <https://doind.gov.np/industrial-property-bulletin> — official publications from 2006 onwards, in Nepali and English.

### Pakistan
- Intellectual Property Organization of Pakistan (IPO-Pakistan): <http://www.ipo.gov.pk> — the authority for patents, trade marks and designs in Pakistan. The website offers policy, regulation and gazette information; the online search service is still to be launched.

### Philippines
- Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL): <http://www.ipophil.gov.ph/index.php> — the Philippine IP authority. Its website offers search entries for patents, utility models and industrial designs, showing basic bibliographic data, with PDF full texts.

### Qatar
- Gulf Cooperation Council Patent Office (GCCPO): <http://www.gccpo.org> — patents in Qatar are granted through the GCC Patent Office. Granted patents from 1998 onwards can be searched, showing bibliographic data, and PDF full texts can generally be downloaded. The interface is in Arabic.

### Saudi Arabia
- Saudi Authority for Intellectual Property (SAIP): <https://www.saip.gov.sa> — the government body responsible for intellectual property in Saudi Arabia; the website is in Arabic and English.
- IP search engine: <https://ipsearch-beta.saip.gov.sa/> — provides patent, design and trade mark databases with bibliographic data, legal status and application and grant dates. Searching by application number, name and classification is supported, along with image search and data download.
- IP gazette: <https://www.saip.gov.sa/publications/> — publishes gazettes and updates on trade marks, patents, industrial designs, integrated circuits and plant varieties.

### Singapore
- Intellectual Property Office of Singapore (IPOS): <https://www.ipos.gov.sg> — a government agency under the Ministry of Law responsible for IP management. The website is in English and provides the IPOS Digital Hub online platform.
- Patent and design search: <https://digitalhub.ipos.gov.sg/FAMN/process/IP4SG/MN_Index> — IPOS Digital Hub covers Singapore patents, designs and trade marks, with patent applications, publications, transactions and application documents. The platform is in English.
- Gazette search: <https://digitalhub.ipos.gov.sg/FAMN/eservice/IP4SG/MN_JournalSearch> — IPOS Digital Hub provides electronic gazette search and download, in English.

### South Korea

- Korea Intellectual Property Rights Information Service (KIPRIS): <https://www.kipris.or.kr/khome/main.do> — operated by the Korea Institute of Patent Information, covering patents, utility models, designs, trade marks and trial information with bibliographic data, legal status, full texts and prosecution information. Fairly international, in Korean and English, though machine translation is charged.
- IP gazette: <https://www.kipris.or.kr/khome/info/statis/todayGb.do> — patent, utility model, design and trade mark gazettes through KIPRIS.

- Official gazette: <http://dcip.gov.sy/disarticle.php?id=110> — recent patent gazettes in Arabic, listing published applications and granted patents.

- Official gazette: <https://im.adliya.uz/publications> — the Uzbek gazette search system, covering patent gazettes from 2006 onwards, in Uzbek and English.

## Europe

### Czechia

### Denmark

### Estonia
- Estonian Patent Office (Patendiamet): <http://www.epa.ee> — the Estonian industrial property authority. Its website provides electronic filing and online services for patents and utility models, and search of bibliographic data and abstracts, but no full texts.

### Finland

### France

### Germany

## General-Purpose Patent Databases and Tools

1. Google Patents: <https://patents.google.com/> — very easy to get started with and fast. It covers patent documents from more than 100 countries and regions, has a table search at <https://patents.google.com/advanced>, supports query building and chemical structure search, and the advanced usage is described in the help page at <https://support.google.com/faqs/answer/7049475>.
1. FreePatentsOnline: <http://www.freepatentsonline.com> — a free patent search and download site covering US, European, Japanese and PCT data with simple statistical analysis. Good enough for everyday use.
1. Patentlens: <http://www.lens.org/lens> — a system built by Bill Gates and his wife's foundation. Note that a foundation means everything is free! Intellectual property needs poverty alleviation from a global perspective. It offers search and download of global patent full texts and links patents to scholarly literature.
1. IP.com: <https://priorart.ip.com> — the best system for defensive publications.
1. RPX Insight: <https://insight.rpxcorp.com/advanced_search> — free but fairly basic; the result list has no abstract drawings for preview.
1. incoPat: <http://www.incopat.com> — from Henghe Zhihui (Beijing), a high-end Chinese patent information database and quite easy to use. It covers more than 200 million patents from 173 countries, regions and organizations — probably the most comprehensive patent database in the world. Expensive, with no free version, but trials are available and generous. Its processed fields are extremely rich, bar none the most field-rich patent database available. Besides conventional search it offers semantic search, smart query building, AI search, graphic search (search by drawing), DWPI data and a patent sandbox. Drawbacks: large areas of wasted screen space on the results page, and the single-page and two-page document views feel disconnected — the single-page view makes it awkward to compare text with drawings, while the two-page view cannot show families, citations or similar documents. Reported many times, never improved.
1. Patentics: <http://www.patentics.com> — Soyi Interactive's semantic search system, covering more than 190 million patents from over 160 countries and regions. Its semantic search technology is well developed and it may be China's most distinctive and forward-looking system, with a search cube, Chinese-English machine translation, family and citation analysis, clustering and abnormal-application screening. Drawbacks: the syntax is hard, as if meant for programmers rather than ordinary users, and the free version only shows the first page of the first result set with limited functions.
1. PatSnap: <https://www.zhihuiya.com> — a Singapore company with offices in Suzhou and the UK. A paid database with relatively high fees and usually short trials; worth applying for a trial. With the rise of AI, PatSnap launched an AI agent (Eureka) that can generate a search query from a passage of text, run it and revise it based on the results — fairly smart, and very suitable both for inventors with no search experience and for experienced patent analysts and IPR teams drafting an initial query. It also offers Patent DNA and specialized databases for new drugs, biological sequences and chemical structures.
1. HimmPat: <https://www.himmpat.com> — a product of Tianqi Heima (Beijing) that came out of beta and started charging on 8 August 2020. Fast, with a good user experience, and it implements most of incoPat's and PatSnap's search functions. Its result list and document view are markedly better than incoPat's — highly recommended. It now covers more than 220 million patents from 172 countries, regions and organizations, plus design data from 80 IP offices, legal status for 150 countries or regions and litigation data for 20 countries, with global semantic search, self-built libraries and monitoring, infringement screening and an AI large model. Available in Search, Analysis, Explorer and Navigator editions, with fairly generous trials.
1. Innojoy: <http://www.innojoy.com> — Dawei Software's patent search engine. Global patents can be searched and read free of charge without registration, and there is a 14-day VIP trial. Optional collections include China, nine countries and two organizations, ASEAN, Belt and Road, IP5, the EU, BRICS and designs. It also offers AI smart search, AI novelty search, AI first-draft disclosure generation and image search; data updates are fairly timely. Fairly recommended.
1. PatentHub: <https://www.patenthub.cn> — a product of Beijing Nanming Technology with both free and paid versions (personal CNY 288/year, professional CNY 980/year, enterprise CNY 6,980/year). The free version works well and looks much like the paid incoPat. It covers more than 140 million patents from 105 countries and regions, with semantic search, semantic clustering (patent maps), custom analysis, image search and batch export. The home page is full of patent listings, mainly granted-but-unpaid patents — people in the field know what those are, so no more said.
1. SooPAT: <http://www.soopat.com> — covers more than 220 million patent documents from 110 countries and regions and supports searching global patents in Chinese. Basic search is free, with table search, IPC classification search, citation search and family search all available, but searching several keywords at once and exporting to Excel or image bundles require a premium membership (coin-based), so it is almost no longer free. Speed has improved considerably compared with earlier years.
1. Baiten: <http://so.baiten.cn> — a product of Changzhou Baiten Technology, covering more than 200 million patents from 108 countries, regions and organizations. Patent search costs CNY 299/year and enterprise search CNY 99/year; trials are available. It offers AI smart search, infringement alerts, novelty comparison, batch export of bibliographic data and full text, foreign-language translation and annuity monitoring. The website experience is so-so.
1. RainPat: <https://www.rainpat.com> — a free database from Jiangsu Runtong, mainly covering Chinese inventions, utility models, designs and granted inventions. Do not make the query too long or nothing will be found. Registration is free and it uses a "Tongqian" credit system; API access requires 10,000 credits and is charged per record. Free batch export is no longer available.
1. Duyan: <http://www.uyanip.com> — a product of Zhejiang Duyan. A free database that works after registration and is reasonably fast. Besides smart and image patent search it also covers trade marks (including Madrid), copyright and geographical indications, plus an agent assistant, infringement comparison, office action reply drafting and disclosure drafting.
1. PatentYee: <http://patyee.com> — a product of Guangzhou OKa Information Consulting. It offers declassified defence patents and requires registration (CARSI institutional accounts and WeChat QR login are supported). A paid database; I have never tried it.
1. DerwentInnovation: <https://www.derwentinnovation.com> — Derwent, said to be as awesome as it is expensive. I tried it briefly and did not find it pleasant to use. A foreign database with no Chinese version, and Chinese patents are machine-translated into English.
1. Questel: <http://www.questel.orbit.com/index.php/en> — the editor has never had the chance to use it. Paid, and family data processing is one of its features.
1. Patbase: <http://www.patbase.com> — a paid database with free trials available, and Chinese, Japanese and English interfaces.
1. WIPS: <http://www.wipsglobal.com/service/mai/main.wips> — a Korean patent search system. Not bad.
1. AcclaimIP: <https://www.acclaimip.com/> — Anaqua's patent search and analysis tool, with semantic search, patent family trees, citation analysis and batch export. Paid, with trials available.
1. Patseer: <http://patseer.com> — being unknown does not mean it is not first class; it is actually a decent product.
1. Patanalyse: <http://www.patanalyse.com> — a British product. Never used it.
1. Sumobrain: <http://www.sumobrain.com> — covers US, European, Japanese and PCT patents. Do people in other countries have no brains?
1. DELPHION: <http://www.delphion.com> — originally IBM's. US patents are free, and it is actually a fairly affordable paid system.
1. MicroPatent: <http://www.micropat.com> — paid, and you can skip it. But it is the birthplace of patent landscape maps.
1. DIALOG: <http://search.proquest.com/professional> — the company has been sold many times; the system is still high-end, paid and very expensive.
1. STN: <https://www.stn.org/stn> — a sharp tool for searching chemistry patents. Forget it if you have no money.
1. CAS (Chemical Abstracts Service): <https://www.cas.org> — no need for much introduction; the name tells you what it is good at.
1. SureChEMBL: <https://www.surechembl.org/> — a free chemical patent database maintained by EMBL-EBI, extracting compound information from the full text, images and attachments of patent documents, and searchable by chemical structure.
1. Reaxys: <https://www.reaxys.com/> — Elsevier's chemistry information platform, covering compounds, reactions and properties from chemical literature and patents. Paid, and commonly used for chemistry patent searching.
1. INSPEC: <http://www.theiet.org/resources/inspec> — a specialist search tool for physics and engineering.
1. Pubmed: <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed> — I studied pharmacy and am used to free things. For people as poor as me, this is absolutely the best choice; look carefully and you will keep finding surprises.

# Classification Search

1. PatentClassMap: <https://patentclassmap.com/> — a classification concordance tool covering IPC, CPC, FI and F-term, with classification titles in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, English, French, German, Spanish and other languages. A Chinese interface is available at <https://patentclassmap.com/zh/>. It runs in the browser, is free and needs no registration, so it is a good place to start when looking up classification symbols.
1. JPO classification concordance tool: <https://www.jpo.go.jp/support/general/searchportal/cgi-bin/search-portal/narabe_tool/narabe_con_en.html> — shows IPC, FI and CPC side by side (only these three schemes are supported now; ECLA and ICO concordances are no longer provided). All classification symbols are given in both English and Japanese, and you can choose to display only part of the symbols or only English or only Japanese. There is no Chinese, but many Japanese terms are Chinese characters, so comparing them with the English is not hard to follow.
1. incoPat's IPC classification search: <http://ipc.incopat.com> — highly recommended. The database is paid but the IPC classification search is free, and it comes in Chinese, English and Japanese versions. Compared with the JPO concordance tool, its shortcoming is that you cannot quickly compare the three language versions side by side.
1. Guangdong IP Public Information Service Platform IPC navigation: <https://s.gpic.gd.cn/access/hostingplatform/nav/route/showNav?industryFlag=0> — shows Chinese patents only.
1. CNIPA IPC classification search: <http://epub.cnipa.gov.cn/ipc> — CNIPA also updates IPC classification documents regularly at <https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/col/col3161/index.html>, where you can extract and print the parts you use most and keep them on your desk. Not recommended for day-to-day classification searching.
1. EPO classification search: <https://worldwide.espacenet.com/classification?locale=en_EP> — in English, updated fairly promptly, and it offers IPC-to-CPC concordance. The experience is excellent. Fairly recommended.
